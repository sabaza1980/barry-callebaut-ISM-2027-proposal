The plenary room

A low set built for a 2.3 m room.

88 m² · 6.13 × 13.68 m

The demonstration room has no height for a raised stage or an LED wall, so we keep everything close to the floor. Management stands on a 10 cm stage in front of a 6 m printed backdrop, with the slides on two 65" screens mounted on the backdrop either side. The room keeps its glass and its character, and the cameras get a calm, consistent background.

Set

A 6 m wide printed backdrop at the end of the room, quoted at 2 m and taken as high as the confirmed ceiling allows. It sits in front of the house 65", so the higher it goes, the cleaner the camera shot. A 4 × 2 m stage, 10 cm high with black carpet and skirt, lifts speakers just enough without eating into the height. Two 65" screens sit on the backdrop left and right of the stage, so the slides stay clear of a presenter at the centre, and the house 65" and 55" return screens carry the same feed.

Daylight

Flat screens instead of projection, four daylight-balanced LED fixtures on stands so speakers look natural on camera, and cameras pointing away from the glazing. The roll-down curtains stay available for direct sun.

Sound

Four headset microphones for the speakers and two handhelds for the closing Q&A, on four compact L-Acoustics speakers: two at the front, two halfway down. With a low ceiling and hard surfaces, that keeps the sound clear and feedback down.

From speeches to Q&A

One presenter at a time on the platform. For the final all-speaker Q&A, the lectern comes off and a row of chairs goes on, with questions taken from the room only.

Digital presentation design

A richer frame for the investor story.

A cocoa-brown surround, warm stone slide cards and a fine red accent give the webcast a considered identity. Layered contours add depth around the content, with a single logo in the header.

All speaker captions read “Placeholder”. Charts are unlabeled samples showing composition, not financial data. Actual slides should be fed directly to the webcast and checked for legibility within the final layout.

Practical delivery choice

Landscape playback is the straightforward option for a standard livestream. Use large slide labels and sparse charts, and offer full-slide views when detailed figures require more space.

Portrait as an additional feature

A portrait viewer can stack slides above a larger speaker panel. This needs separate synchronized slide and camera feeds and support from the webcast provider; ordinary widescreen video does not rearrange itself into this layout.

Afternoon · Gourmet brands

Five breakouts, one rotation.

After lunch, guests split into five groups of about twelve and rotate through five rooms, one Gourmet brand per room, with a demonstration and a tasting in each. Everyone sees every brand, the rooms never overflow, and the afternoon ends on time for the 17:00 coaches.
