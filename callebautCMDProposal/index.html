Recommended direction · simplified option

One straight backdrop. A clear view of management.

Low–medium relative cost

The warm-gray backdrop gives speakers a calm, consistent background. Two screens carry the presentation, while the room’s glass and heritage setting remain visible at the edges.

Room orientation

The stage sits parallel to the heritage-facing glazed wall. The coffee bar’s fixed oak canopy is outside the main view. The original exposed ceiling, glazing and architecture remain in place; there is no added overhead scenery.

Daylight control

An opaque freestanding flat prevents bright windows from appearing behind speakers. Removable solar-control film reduces side-window glare. Soft lights on floor stands illuminate faces consistently; additional plain flats can mask direct sun where needed. No curtains.

From speeches to discussion

Keep the stage clear for each presentation. Bring on two low armchairs for a relaxed conversation, or two backed high stools for a shorter interview with a higher seated sightline.

For the final all-speaker Q&A, park the lectern offstage and bring forward five to seven compact chairs. The two-chair scenes supplement that final Q&A arrangement.

Cost approach

Standard rental staging, screens and fabric flats do most of the work. Window treatment and lighting deserve priority over decorative construction.

Digital presentation design

A richer frame for the investor story.

A cocoa-brown surround, warm stone slide cards and a fine red accent give the webcast a considered identity. Layered contours add depth around the content, with a single logo in the header.

All speaker captions read “Placeholder”. Charts are unlabeled samples showing composition, not financial data. Actual slides should be fed directly to the webcast and checked for legibility within the final layout.

Practical delivery choice

Landscape playback is the straightforward option for a standard livestream. Use large slide labels and sparse charts, and offer full-slide views when detailed figures require more space.

Portrait as an additional feature

A portrait viewer can stack slides above a larger speaker panel. This needs separate synchronized slide and camera feeds and support from the webcast provider; ordinary widescreen video does not rearrange itself into this layout.

Recommendation

The simplified backdrop, with low chairs and the developed webcast graphics.

The room stays professional and easy to operate. An opaque background gives the cameras a reliable view of management, while the webcast receives the additional design detail. Use split-screen for narrative passages and the slide-led inset for financial presentations.

Production approach

Designed for a short build and a long morning.

Space & camera

Plan 60 theatre seats in two blocks, with clear aisles and a rear camera sightline. Reserve space for controls and displaced lounge furniture. Confirm spacing and exits against the measured venue plan.

Light & content

Rehearse in the morning light. Check standing and seated faces, the densest slide from the back row, and webcast readability. For LED, test camera settings for banding and moiré.

Build & removal

Use pre-sized rental components and preconfigured graphics. All added equipment is floor-supported below 3 m. Allow sufficient crew and access for a one-day build and same-evening strike.
